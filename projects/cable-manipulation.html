Detecting singularities in dual-arm cable manipulation

Institut Pascal · Research internship · 18 May – 18 September 2026
Role
Research intern
Duration
May – Sep 2026 (4 months)
Platform
2× Franka FR3 + OptiTrack
Stack

01

Project description

The shape of a cable or rod depends both on how the robot moves it and on the object's own mechanics. During my internship I studied whether the configurations where shape control breaks down can be detected directly from experimental data, without a model of the object.

02

What I did

Closed-loop shape-servoing pipeline

Built on a dual-arm Franka FR3 platform, tracked by a 15-camera OptiTrack system and running on ROS2.

Online deformation-Jacobian estimation

Finite-difference least-squares regression, with optional Broyden rank-one updates between re-estimations.

SVD diagnostics

Singular values, rank and condition number, used to detect and classify each type of singularity.

Open-loop stress experiments

Slack, taut, symmetric deformation, buckling, snap-through and torsion, each designed to push the object towards one type of singularity.

03

Three ways control can fail

ControllabilityAn actuation direction stops having an effect on the shape.
ObservabilityThe object deforms, but the chosen features don't register it.
MechanicalBuckling or snap-through moves the object to a new equilibrium.

Key finding: rank alone is a poor early warning. The Jacobian can stay full rank while controllability is already almost lost.

Experiment explorer

Real robot footage · logged diagnostics
Baseline

Both arms in closed loop with Broyden updates. The tracking error converges to near zero and the Jacobian rank stays full throughout. This is the reference behaviour the singular cases are compared against.

Controllability

Pushing the cable ends together until it goes slack. The smallest singular value collapses by more than an order of magnitude (≈0.9 → 0.03) while the rank stays at 3, so rank checks alone would miss it.

Controllability

Pulling the cable taut. As it straightens, σmin falls to ~5% of its plateau value and the reachable shape-change volume shrinks. The driver's auto-stop fires on this signal. Shown here with the automatic singularity-taxonomy card.

Observability

Moving both grippers symmetrically. ≈81% of the real rod-orientation change is invisible to the point features, so the rank briefly drops. This is an observability failure, distinct from a controllability one.

Mechanical

Compressing the rod until it buckles. Two of the three singular values collapse sharply at the transition, as the local Jacobian model breaks down when the rod jumps to a new equilibrium.

Mechanical

Rotating one gripper until the rod snaps through. The rank drops from 3 to 2 at the snap and stays there, and the features jump in a single step.

Rank loss

Twisting both grippers about the vertical axis. The singular values step down as the twist builds, and the Jacobian loses a rank (6 → 5) late in the motion.

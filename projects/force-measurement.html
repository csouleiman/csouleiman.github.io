01

Project description

Taking post-consumer shoes apart by hand is “technically difficult and time consuming” (Lee & Rahimifard, 2012), which is one reason so few shoes get recycled. Before a robot can separate a shoe, we need to know how much force it takes and how the shoe actually comes apart.

Our system uses two UR3e arms to pull the sole away from the upper, measures the force needed, and records a dataset of motions, forces and torques for every pull.

02

What the research says

There is no single “separation force”

  • ISO 17708, the factory test for sole bonding, separates three ways a pull can end: the glue lets go, the sole splits, or the material tears. We label every trial with this taxonomy.
  • The force depends on the pull speed and the conditions, so we vary the speed from trial to trial.

Fix one arm, pull with the other

  • Sako et al. (2025) used a dual-arm “fix-and-disassemble” strategy: one arm grasps and locks the object, the other pulls.
  • With fixation the object moved less, and success rose to 100% against 80% for the baseline. Our holder / puller split follows the same idea.

Sources: Lee & Rahimifard, Resources, Conservation and Recycling, 2012 · ISO 17708:2018 · arXiv:2107.09367 · Sako et al., 2025.

03

System design

UR1 · the holder shoe tree

  • An adjustable 3D-printed shoe tree customised to our shoe. It goes inside the shoe and spreads to hold it.
  • A mount for the UR3e flange (ISO 9409-1-50-4-M6), a stem through the shoe collar, and a clamp on the shoe tree's rail.
  • The spring is replaced by a fixed spacer, so the shoe cannot move while it is pulled.

UR2 · the puller OnRobot 2FG7

  • The gripper grips the sole edge at the toe, with a fixed pre-opening.
  • It pulls in a straight line at constant speed while the force at the wrist is recorded.
04

Peel simulation

The full sequence runs in a simulated two-arm cell (ROS2, MoveIt2, simulated hardware and cameras) before it goes on the real robots.

05

The dataset

Every 10 ms

  • Pull force at the wrist
  • Gripper position along the pull
  • Time

Once per trial

  • Sole separated or not, and how (ISO 17708 failure mode)
  • Peak force (N)
  • Distance to separation

Autonomous shoe inspection on a UR3e

Role
Team lead
Team

01

Project description

For a cross-border remanufacturing initiative, I led an 8-person team that took a defect-inspection pipeline from Gazebo to a real UR3e with a wrist-mounted RealSense camera. The work covered calibration, perception, motion planning and deep-learning defect detection.

02

Pipeline

Hand-eye calibration

Tsai-Lenz with an ArUco marker, for the camera-to-tool0 transform. A first attempt was rejected; I traced it to wrong marker parameters and fixed it.

Shoe localisation

A depth-based node: floor-plane estimation, object masking, contour filtering and 3D projection.

Visual servoing

A finite-state machine with P-control on the pixel error, to centre the shoe in the camera view.

5-view inspection

Viewpoints defined in a shoe-centric frame through TF2, reached with collision-aware MoveIt2 / RRT* planning, an attached camera-mount collision volume, retry-on-failure logic and velocity capped at 15%.

Defect detection

A two-stage CNN: U-Net segmentation to locate defects, then a classifier that labels each one as a hole or a scratch.

03

What I learned on the real robot

  • The model weights would not load when moved from Windows to Linux, so I retrained the full model stack natively on Ubuntu.
  • The model validated cleanly on smartphone images, then I diagnosed a domain-shift gap on the robot's own camera. Perspective, lighting and unseen colours caused specific, reproducible failures, not random errors.
  • I delivered a roadmap to close the gap: augmenting the data with robot-camera captures, yaw-aligned viewpoints and hardware with more reach.

Teaching a TurtleBot3 to navigate with deep RL

01

Project description

Our team implemented Policy Gradient, DQN and Actor-Critic from scratch in PyTorch and trained a TurtleBot3 to avoid obstacles from raw LiDAR. I owned the environment and reward design, the debugging of Policy Gradient and DQN, and the curriculum-learning extension.

02

Setup

PartDesign
State24-ray LiDAR (every 15th ray, clipped to 3.5 m)
Actions3 discrete moves: forward, turn left, turn right
Reward+1 open space · −1 near obstacle · −200 on collision
CurriculumStage 1 with no obstacles, then Stage 2 with obstacles, starting from the Stage 1 weights. 26-D state, 5 actions, dense goal-progress reward
03

What I did

Fixed a zero-variance reward crash

The robot was respawning against a wall, which silently corrupted Policy Gradient training. Forcing /reset_simulation every episode fixed it.

Repaired the DQN

Fixed inherited bugs in the replay buffer and replaced the sparse reward with a graduated, distance-based signal.

Built the curriculum-learning DQN

Goal-conditioned, staged Gazebo environments with a distributed ROS2 service architecture and a dense reward function.

Benchmarked all three algorithms

Head-to-head test episodes to isolate what actually drove success.

04

Results

Success rate by algorithm

Key insight

Same core DQN, very different outcome. Staging the task let the agent learn to reach the goal before it ever met obstacles, which shows that curriculum learning was the deciding factor, not architecture or reward tuning alone.

Actor-Critic was the top performer overall at 70% success, thanks to variance-reduced policy updates.
